# NutriPlan — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Health-conscious individuals in India (25–45) who are managing specific health conditions (PCOS, thyroid, anaemia, diabetes) alongside their nutrition goals. They may also be fitness-oriented people looking to bulk, cut, or maintain. Used primarily on mobile during meal-planning sessions (Sunday evenings, Monday mornings) and at mealtimes to check what to eat next.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Grounded. Trusted. Warm.

Not a tech startup — more like a knowledgeable nutritionist who has great taste. Feels like getting a personalised recommendation from someone who genuinely knows both food science and Indian cooking.

Anti-references: Avoid anything that feels like a "wellness app" startup (gradient pills, glowing rings, purple/teal AI palette). Avoid clinical cold whiteness. Avoid generic food stock photography aesthetics.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light mode primary (users consult during the day, at mealtimes, in daylight — not in dark offices). Dark mode supported.
- **Color**: Warm forest green (oklch 152° hue) as brand. Not mint, not teal. Warm amber accent (oklch 52°) used very sparingly. Neutrals tinted toward brand hue.
- **Typography**: Bricolage Grotesque (display/headings — geometric, warm, eccentric) + Noto Serif (body editorial text). Both chosen to feel warm but confident, unlike generic sans.
- **Composition**: Left-aligned, asymmetric. Editorial rather than marketing. Big numbers, large type contrasts.
- **Layout**: Numbered features, editorial whitespace, full-width section breaks using color.

### Design Principles
1. **Trust through calm** — The interface should feel like an expert, not a salesperson. Restrained, confident, purposeful.
2. **Numbers first** — Data is the product. Make macros, calories, and progress the heroes — not decorative icons.
3. **Cultural respect** — Indian recipes and health traditions are not footnotes. They are the main feature.
4. **Hierarchy through contrast** — Use dramatic font-weight contrast (800 vs 400) and size contrast (3xl vs xs) rather than color to create hierarchy.
5. **Every color earns its place** — Green means brand/success. Amber means warning/accent. No decorative gradients.
